Subscription revenue waterfall template

This free workbook shows how monthly recurring revenue moves from the start of each month to the end: new customers, expansion and price increases come in, churn and contraction go out, and the ending figure carries forward. It also calculates a revenue forecast, net and gross revenue retention, ARR and customer counts for twelve months.

How does the waterfall work, line by line?

The model has one sheet, with January through December across the top and a Notes column on the right that explains every row. These are the lines that make up the waterfall:

LineHow the workbook calculates it
Beginning MRREach month starts with the previous month's ending MRR; January starts from the Starting MRR input.
New customer MRRNew customers multiplied by the annual contract value (ACV), divided by 12.
Expansion MRRExpansion rate applied to beginning MRR. New-customer revenue is counted separately.
Price increasesThe annual price increase converted to an equivalent monthly compounded rate, applied to beginning MRR.
Churned MRRCustomer churn rate applied to beginning MRR (assumes revenue churns in proportion to customers).
Contraction MRRDowngrades on beginning MRR, separate from customer churn.
Net new MRRTotal additions minus total losses.
Ending MRRBeginning MRR plus net new MRR. This becomes next month's beginning MRR.

What do you enter?

Blue cells are inputs and dark type is formulas. The Assumptions block at the top holds starting MRR, monthly customer churn, new customer annual contract value, new customers in January, monthly growth in new customer additions, monthly expansion and contraction rates, an annual price increase, and the starting customer count.

Below the waterfall, a revenue timing block applies a monthly seasonality factor and signed adjustments for implementation delays, revenue-recognition timing and sales ramp to produce a planning revenue forecast.

What does it report?

  • Month-over-month MRR growth and year-end MRR growth.
  • Net revenue retention and gross revenue retention.
  • ARR (annual run rate), calculated as ending MRR multiplied by 12.
  • Cumulative revenue year to date and the annual revenue forecast.
  • New customers added, ending customers and MRR per ending customer.

Frequently asked questions

What is a subscription revenue waterfall in this template?

It is a month-by-month bridge from beginning monthly recurring revenue (MRR) to ending MRR. Additions (new customers, expansion and price increases) are added, losses (churn and contraction) are subtracted, and the ending figure carries into the next month.

Which inputs do I change?

The blue cells in the Assumptions block: starting MRR, monthly customer churn, new customer ACV, new customers in January, monthly acquisition growth, expansion and contraction rates, the annual price increase and starting customers. Dark type is formulas.

How is the revenue forecast different from ending MRR?

The revenue forecast row multiplies ending MRR by an editable seasonality factor and adds signed adjustments for implementation delays, revenue-recognition timing and sales ramp. It is a planning estimate, not contract-level revenue recognition.

What do net and gross revenue retention measure here?

Net revenue retention includes churn, contraction, expansion and price changes and excludes new customers. Gross revenue retention includes only churn and contraction. ARR in the workbook is ending MRR multiplied by 12, not annual recognized revenue.

Are the numbers in the workbook real?

No. The workbook is an illustrative USD model with sample assumptions. Replace them with your own figures before using it for a decision.
